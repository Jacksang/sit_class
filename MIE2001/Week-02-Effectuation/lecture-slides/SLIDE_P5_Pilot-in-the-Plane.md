## Slide P5-1 — Principle 5: Pilot-in-the-Plane Intro

**Text:**
```
✈️  Principle 5: Pilot-in-the-Plane

    "Focus on what you CAN control,
     not on predicting the future"

    Tagline: Don't forecast. Act.

    The causation trap:
    ❌ "Let me predict the market → then decide"
    ❌ Paralysis by analysis

    The effectual approach:
    ✅ "What can I control RIGHT NOW?"
    ✅ Act → Learn → Adjust → Repeat
```

**Speaker Notes:**
> "Principle five: Pilot-in-the-Plane. Focus on what you can control, not on predicting the future.
>
> The causation trap is thinking you need to predict the market before you act. That leads to paralysis by analysis.
>
> Expert entrepreneurs focus on what they can control right now. Act first. Learn from the action. Adjust and repeat."

**Image Prompt:** A simple airplane cockpit silhouette. The pilot facing forward, hands on controls. Minimalist line art. Blue accent.

## Slide P5-2 — Control vs. Prediction Mindset

**Text:**
```
✈️  What You Control vs. What You Don't

    ┌──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┐
    │  ❌ I CANNOT CONTROL │  ✅ I CAN CONTROL     │
    ├──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┤
    │ The economy          │ My effort today       │
    │ My competitors       │ Who I talk to         │
    │ Market trends        │ What I build this wk  │
    │ VC funding cycles    │ How I spend my time   │
    │ Customer decisions   │ What questions I ask  │
    │ Luck                 │ How I respond to bad  │
    │                      │ news                  │
    └──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┘

    🔑 "Predictions are guesses.
       Actions are facts."
```

**Speaker Notes:**
> "Let's be honest about what you can and cannot control.
>
> You can't control the economy, your competitors, market trends, or VC funding cycles. If you wait for all of those to be perfect — you'll wait forever.
>
> But you CAN control your effort today. Who you talk to. What you build this week. How you spend your time.
>
> Predictions are guesses. Actions are facts. Choose facts."

## Slide P5-3 — Pilot-in-the-Plane Examples

**Text:**
```
✈️  Pilot-in-the-Plane in Action

    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │ 🇸🇬 Grab:                                  │
    │   Couldn't predict if drivers would       │
    │   adopt a booking app                     │
    │   Controlled: signed ONE driver           │
    │   → Tested dispatch with 10 calls/day    │
    │   → Proved demand → Scaled                │
    │                                          │
    │ 🌍 Airbnb:                                │
    │   Couldn't predict if strangers would     │
    │   share living space                      │
    │   Controlled: built ONE listing           │
    │   → Took photos themselves                │
    │   → Stayed with first guests              │
    │                                          │
    │ 🧑‍🎓 You:                                  │
    │   Can't predict if your idea will work    │
    │   Can control: interview 5 people TOMORROW│
    └──────────────────────────────────────────┘

    💡 Start with one unit of action.
       Prove it. Then scale.
```

**Speaker Notes:**
> "Grab: Anthony Tan couldn't predict if Malaysian taxi drivers would adopt a booking app. So he didn't try. He controlled what he could — he signed one driver, tested the dispatch system, and proved demand.
>
> Airbnb: Brian Chesky couldn't predict if strangers would rent out their homes. He controlled what he could — he built one listing, took the photos himself, and stayed with the first guests to learn.
>
> For you: you can't predict if your idea will work. But you CAN control interviewing 5 people tomorrow."

## Slide P5-5 — Why Small Actions Compound

**Text:**
```
✈️  Action Beats Prediction

    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │ Week 1: "I'll interview 3 potential customers"   │
    │   → Learn they don't care about your feature     │
    │   → Pivot before building anything               │
    │                                                  │
    │ Week 2: "I'll test a different angle"             │
    │   → 2 out of 3 are interested now                │
    │   → You're onto something                        │
    │                                                  │
    │ Week 3: "I'll build the simplest MVP"             │
    │   → First user signs up                          │
    │   → They tell their friends                      │
    │                                                  │
    │ vs. Spending 3 months on a business plan         │
    │ that nobody reads                                │
    └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

    💡 The pilot doesn't predict the weather.
       They adjust the controls moment by moment.
```

**Speaker Notes:**
> "Look at the difference. In 3 weeks of action — interviews, pivots, a simple MVP — you get real feedback. Real data. Real progress.
>
> vs. spending 3 months writing a business plan that nobody reads and that's based on guesses.
>
> A pilot doesn't predict the weather. They just keep adjusting the controls moment by moment. That's what entrepreneurs do."
