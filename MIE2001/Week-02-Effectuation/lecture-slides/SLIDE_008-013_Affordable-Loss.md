## Slide 10 — Principle 2: Affordable Loss (Deep Dive, 5 min)

**Text:**
```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                                                      ║
║      💰  Principle 2: Affordable Loss                 ║
║                                                      ║
║   "Invest only what you can afford to lose"          ║
║                                                      ║
║   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐   ║
║   │   ❌ Traditional thinking:                    │   ║
║   │   "How much could I MAKE?"                   │   ║
║   │   → Project millions → Overspend → Burn      │   ║
║   └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘   ║
║                                                      ║
║   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐   ║
║   │   ✅ Effectual thinking:                      │   ║
║   │   "What's the MOST I could LOSE?"             │   ║
║   │   → Cap downside → Take smart risks → Go     │   ║
║   └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘   ║
║                                                      ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

**Speaker Notes:**
> "Principle two: Affordable Loss. 'Invest only what you can afford to lose.'
>
> The traditional approach is: 'How much could I make?' If the projected return is big enough, go for it.
>
> That's how people end up spending $50,000 on a business that fails. Because the projected returns looked amazing — but the downside was real.
>
> Expert entrepreneurs flip the question. They ask: 'What's the most I could lose?'
>
> They calculate the worst case. If they can survive it, they go ahead. If they can't, they don't.
>
> This is not risk aversion. This is intelligent risk-taking."

**Image Prompt:** Simple scale illustration. Left: pile of gold coins with "?" — "How much could I MAKE?" Right: small limit line — "What can I LOSE?" Minimalist line art.

**Speaker Notes:**
> "Here's the thing about Affordable Loss — there's no universal number.
>
> Someone in this room might have $5,000 they can afford to risk. Someone else might have $50. Both are valid.
>
> The point isn't the number. The point is knowing your number.
>
> Money: whatever you can lose without losing sleep.
> Time: whatever you can give without grades suffering.
> Ego: whatever discomfort you can handle without giving up.
>
> When you define YOUR affordable loss — whether it's $50 or $5,000 — something shifts. You stop worrying about the worst case because you've already mapped it out. And you know you can survive it.
>
> That's when you become free to start."
