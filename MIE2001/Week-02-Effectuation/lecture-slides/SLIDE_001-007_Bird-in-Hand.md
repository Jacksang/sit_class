## Slide 3 — Sarasvathy's Discovery (2 min)

**Text:**
```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                                                      ║
║   📊 How Expert Entrepreneurs Actually Think         ║
║                                                      ║
║   Saras Sarasvathy — University of Virginia          ║
║                                                      ║
║   Studied: 27 expert entrepreneurs                   ║
║   (15+ years experience, multiple exits)             ║
║                                                      ║
║   Result: Experts don't think like managers           ║
║                                                      ║
║   ┌─────────────┐       ┌──────────────────┐         ║
║   │  Causation  │  vs   │  Effectuation    │         ║
║   │ (Novices)   │       │  (Experts)       │         ║
║   │ Goal → Plan │       │ Means → Create   │         ║
║   │ → Execute   │       │ → Iterate        │         ║
║   └─────────────┘       └──────────────────┘         ║
║                                                      ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

**Speaker Notes:**
> "Sarasvathy called this contrast causation versus effectuation.
>
> The causation path — the one most people take — goes: 'I have a goal. Let me make a plan to achieve it. Then I'll execute.'
>
> But expert entrepreneurs use the other path. They start with the means they have and ask: 'What can I create with these means?'
>
> Both approaches are valid. Causation is great for predictable situations. But entrepreneurship is deeply unpredictable. And for unpredictable situations, effectuation is not just better — it's the only way that reliably works."

**Image Prompt:** Two simple flowcharts side by side. Left: Goal → Plan → Execute (straight line). Right: Means → Create → Iterate (circular arrow). Minimalist.


**Speaker Notes:**
> "Let's start with Principle 1: Bird-in-Hand. 'Start with who you are, what you know, and who you know.'
>
> Sounds simple — almost obvious. But think about how deeply counterintuitive this is. Every business book tells you: research first, plan first, get money first, then start.
>
> Expert entrepreneurs do the opposite. They look in the mirror — and into their phone contacts — and ask: 'What do I already have?'
>
> Three categories:
> - Who you are: Your skills, experience, personality.
> - What you know: The domains, industries, hobbies you genuinely understand.
> - Who you know: Your network. Not CEOs of Fortune 500 companies — your classmates, your professors, your family."

**Image Prompt:** A three-column simple diagram. Left: person icon (WHO I AM). Center: brain/lightbulb (WHAT I KNOW). Right: network nodes (WHO I KNOW). Minimalist.

## Slide 7 — Your Bird-in-Hand: 60 seconds (Interactive)

**Text:**
```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                                                      ║
║     ✏️ Your Bird-in-Hand: 60 seconds                 ║
║                                                      ║
║     "What is ONE resource you have right now         ║
║      that could be the starting point of             ║
║      a venture?"                                     ║
║                                                      ║
║        It could be:                                   ║
║        • 🟦 Skills — code, design, write, speak      ║
║        • 🟩 Knowledge — an industry you understand   ║
║        • 🟥 Network — someone who could help          ║
║        • 🟨 Assets — a tool, space, platform          ║
║                                                      ║
║        ⏱ 60 seconds — write it down.                 ║
║          Be real, not perfect.                       ║
║                                                      ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

**Speaker Notes:**
> "I want you to take out your phone — or grab a piece of paper. One question. 60 seconds.
>
> 'What is ONE resource you have right now — one skill, one connection, anything — that could be the starting point of a venture?'
>
> It doesn't have to be a billion-dollar idea. It doesn't have to be sexy. It just has to be real.
>
> 60 seconds. Go."
>
> At 30s: "Thirty seconds — if you're stuck, think about the last thing a friend asked you to help them with."
> At 10s: "Ten seconds — push yourself to write something down."
> At time: "OK, time's up. Hold onto that."
>
> Ask 1-2 people to share if comfortable. Otherwise, share your own example. Then: "Nobody wrote 'I have nothing.' That's the point."
