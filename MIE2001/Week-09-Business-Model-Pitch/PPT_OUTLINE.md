## Part 2: BMC Deep Dive — All 9 Blocks (30 min)

### Slide 4 — Block 1: Customer Segments
**Question:** Who are we creating value for?
- Be specific (from Week 7 segmentation work)
- "Everyone" is not an answer

### Slide 5 — Block 2: Value Propositions
**Question:** What value do we deliver?
- Types: newness, performance, customization, price, convenience, etc.
- **Exercise:** "In one sentence, why choose YOU over alternatives?"

### Slide 6 — Block 3: Channels
**Question:** How do we reach customers?
- Your top 3 channels from Week 7
- Map to: awareness, evaluation, purchase, delivery, after-sales

### Slide 7 — Block 4: Customer Relationships
**Question:** What relationship does each segment expect?
- Types: personal, self-service, automated, community, co-creation
- **Key question:** "Is this scalable?"

### Slide 8 — Block 5: Revenue Streams
**Question:** How do customers pay?
- Your revenue model from Week 8
- Types: asset sale, subscription, freemium, advertising, marketplace, licensing
- **Check:** Does it match willingness to pay?

### Slide 9 — Block 6: Key Resources
**Question:** What unique resources do we need?
- Physical, intellectual, human, financial
- **Student reality:** Your TIME and TEAM are your key resources

### Slide 10 — Block 7: Key Activities
**Question:** What are the most important things we DO?
- Production, problem-solving, platform/network
- **Check:** What do you spend most time on? That should be your key activity

### Slide 11 — Block 8: Key Partnerships
**Question:** Who can help us deliver value?
- Crazy Quilt in practice (from Week 2)
- Singapore partners: SIT I&E, alumni, industry connections

### Slide 12 — Block 9: Cost Structure
**Question:** What are the most important costs?
- From your financial model (Week 8)
- Fixed vs. variable, cost-driven vs. value-driven

### Slide 13 — Coherence Check
**The most important question:** Do all 9 blocks tell ONE consistent story?

| Customer | Value | Cost | Revenue |
|----------|-------|------|---------|
| Premium | Premium | High cost | High price ✓ |
| Premium | Premium | Low cost | Low price ✗ |

**Your turn:** Read your BMC left-to-right. Does it make sense?

## Part 5: The Pitch Deck (20 min)

### Slide 21 — The Purpose of a Pitch Deck
- **NOT** to explain everything about your business
- **YES** to make them *want* to learn more
- **Guy Kawasaki's 10/20/30 Rule:** 10 slides, 20 minutes, 30-point font
- For final presentations: **10 slides max, 7 minutes**

### Slide 22 — The 10-Slide Structure
| # | Slide | BMC Block |
|---|-------|-----------|
| 1 | Title + Tagline | — |
| 2 | The Problem | Customer Segments, Value Prop |
| 3 | The Solution | Value Proposition |
| 4 | Market Size | Customer Segments |
| 5 | Traction | Key Activities, Partnerships |
| 6 | Business Model | Revenue Streams, Cost Structure |
| 7 | Competition | Value Prop, Relationships |
| 8 | Team | Key Resources |
| 9 | Financials | Revenue, Cost |
| 10 | The Ask | — |

### Slide 23 — Slide-by-Slide Quick Reference
- **Problem:** Start with story or statistic. Make pain tangible.
- **Solution:** Show, don't tell (screenshots, demo). Focus on benefits, not features.
- **Market:** TAM → SAM → SOM. Show you're realistic.
- **Traction:** Customer interviews count. MVP results count. Revenue is gold.
- **Business Model:** Pricing, CAC, LTV. Keep it simple.
- **Competition:** Direct + Indirect matrix. Your unique advantage.
- **Team:** Photos + 1-line bios. Show complementary skills.
- **Financials:** 3-year simplified. Break-even point.
- **The Ask:** Specific. Achievable. "We're raising XXX to achieve YYY by ZZZ."

### Slide 24 — Good Deck vs. Bad Deck
| Bad Deck | Good Deck |
|----------|-----------|
| 20+ slides | 10 slides |
| Tiny text | 30+ pt font |
| Lots of words | Mostly visuals |
| No flow | Clear story arc |
| "We're a platform..." | "We help [customer]..." |
| No traction | Real data, even small |

## Part 6: Storytelling (15 min)

### Slide 25 — Data Without Story is Boring
- **Formula:** Emotional hook + Logical argument = Compelling pitch
- **Structure:** Beginning (problem) → Middle (journey) → End (vision)

### Slide 26 — The Hero's Journey for Pitches
**Customer is the hero. You're the guide.**

- **Hero:** Customer living normally
- **Villain:** The problem
- **Guide:** Your solution
- **Plan:** How it works
- **Happy ending:** Life is better

### Slide 27 — Storytelling Workshop (10 min)
**3-sentence pitch story:**
1. "Before [solution], [customer] struggled with [problem]..."
2. "We built [solution] to solve this..."
3. "Now, [customer] can [benefit]..."

**Example:** "Before LunchMate, SIT students wasted 15 minutes daily on lunch decisions. We built a simple app that shows the best option in 30 seconds. Now, students save 60 hours per semester."

## Part 7: Handling Q&A (10 min)

### Slide 28 — Common Questions
| Question | What They Mean | How to Answer |
|----------|---------------|---------------|
| "Why will this work?" | What evidence? | Share traction |
| "Who else does this?" | Understand competition? | Show matrix |
| "How make money?" | Real business? | Show unit economics |
| "Biggest risk?" | Are you honest? | Name it + mitigation plan |
| "Why YOUR team?" | Right skills? | Show complementary expertise |

### Slide 29 — Handling Tough Questions
1. Don't lie or exaggerate
2. "I don't know" is OK — follow with "but I'll find out"
3. Redirect to your strength
4. Practice the 5 hardest questions
